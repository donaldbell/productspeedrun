Gamer Companion

The monitor-top robot that keeps you in peak performance.

It notices when you sit down, then nudges you to take a break, drink some water, grab a snack or sit up straight. It uses a puff of air and a friendly voice, so you never have to look away from the game.

Who it's for

Two people, one desk

The user · 12 to 15

The gamer

Headphones on and eyes locked on the screen. Three hours vanish, the water bottle is still full and their back is a question mark. A chibi robot that looks like it belongs next to their RGB keyboard is something they'll actually leave on the monitor.

The buyer

The parent

They've asked nicely through the door four times. Gamer Companion repeats those reminders from inside the kid's own setup, in a voice that isn't Mum's or Dad's, and it doesn't need a camera in the bedroom to do it.

The moment: 90 minutes into a ranked match, a cool breeze hits your face and a small voice says "Grab some water. You've got this."

Try it

A nudge you feel, not one you watch

Pick a reminder. The robot's eyes light up in that reminder's colour, it speaks one short line and then the breeze kicks in. Turn voice on to hear it in your browser's own speech voice. The real robot plays recorded phrases.

Trigger a reminder

Each reminder has its own eye colour, so a glance from the corner of your eye is enough.

On the real robot, saying "done", "snooze" or "I drank water" is heard by a wake word and fixed-command model that runs on the chip. No audio leaves the device.

Usable without looking at it

Three cues, ranked by how rude they are

01 · AIR

A breeze on your face

A 30 mm blower behind the mouth grille puffs a cool breeze at you for a few seconds. You feel it through a headset, mid-fight, without it covering any game audio. It runs at a low PWM duty so it whispers rather than whines.

02 · VOICE

One short line

A 28 mm speaker on the side plays one stored phrase: "Time for a break! Stretch your body." It's loud enough to get past headphones at speech level, and short enough not to break your concentration.

03 · LIGHT

Eyes, only when needed

Two RGB LEDs behind the smoked visor stay dark the rest of the time. When a reminder fires they glow in its colour, so peripheral vision tells you which one it was. They never blink on just to look pretty.

Privacy is part of the design. Presence comes from a passive infrared sensor, the same kind used in a hallway light, so it knows someone is there but not who. The microphone listens for a wake word and a short list of commands, all on the ESP32-S3 itself. There's no camera, no cloud account and no recordings.

This is the first-pass CAD model, with every dimension taken from real, cheap parts. Drag to spin it. It has a face cap and a rear shell, a smoked PMMA visor, neon side rings, TPU feet and a hinged clip that hooks down the back of the monitor.

Stage 5 · KiCad

What's inside

One 48 × 31 mm two-layer board stands upright behind the face. The front of the board is what sees out through the visor: the PIR and the two eye LEDs. The back carries the ESP32-S3, the audio and the power parts. The blower, the speaker and the USB-C socket connect with short JST-SH leads.

U2 · ESP32-S3-WROOM-1-N16R8

The brain: ESP32-S3, 16 MB flash + 8 MB PSRAM

We picked the S3 because it's the ESP32 that can run Espressif's ESP-SR speech stack (a wake word plus about 200 fixed commands) on the chip itself. It also has two I²S ports, one for the mic and one for the amp, and native USB, so it flashes over the same cable that powers it.

U5 · AM312

Presence

A tiny 3 V PIR draws about 15 µA. Its dome pokes through the visor, and it gets its own RC-filtered supply so fan and Wi-Fi noise don't cause false triggers.

U4 · ICS-43434

Ears

A digital I²S MEMS mic, ported through a 1 mm hole in the board to a pinhole in the face.

U3 · MAX98357A + LS1

Voice

A class-D I²S amp on 5 V drives a 28 mm, 8 Ω, 1 W speaker. It needs no codec and no filter.

Q1 · AO3400A + 3010 blower

Breeze

A low-side MOSFET switches the blower at 25 kHz PWM, which is too high to hear. A flyback diode protects it, and a pull-down keeps the fan off at boot.

LED1–2 · WS2812B-2020

Eyes

Two 2 mm RGB LEDs on one data line. One diode drops their supply to about 4.3 V, so the 3.3 V data signal reads reliably and no level shifter is needed.

U1 · AP2112K-3.3

Power

USB-C 5 V in, with a 600 mA LDO for the 3.3 V rail. There's no battery, so nothing ever needs charging.

Power budget on 5 V

The green bar is typical draw and the pale bar is peak. The orange line marks the 500 mA a USB 2.0 port promises.

If everything peaked at once it would draw about 870 mA, so the firmware takes turns: speech first, then the breeze, with Wi-Fi off. On a USB-C charger, the 5.1 kΩ CC resistors unlock 1.5 A anyway.

What's next

What we still don't know

This is a speedrun first pass. Here's what we'd want to answer before anyone orders a board or cuts a mould.

Board

Four connections are still unrouted

The last routing run left PIR power, two I²S amp lines and the amp's shutdown pin unconnected (ERC is clean, DRC isn't). The placement around the amp needs loosening and another routing run.

Brief vs. reality

Whisper won't fit on an ESP32

The brief asked for Whisper speech-to-text, but even Whisper-tiny needs far more RAM than the S3 has. A wake word plus fixed commands covers "snooze", "done" and "I drank water". Free-form speech would mean an ESP32-P4 or a small Linux chip, which brings a bigger board and more heat.

Feel

Can you feel the breeze at arm's length?

3010 blowers move very little air. We need to test whether it's noticeable at 50–70 cm on a low, quiet duty cycle. If it isn't, the fix is a 40 mm axial fan and a bigger head.

Sensing

Does the PIR see a still, seated gamer?

PIRs detect movement, and a focused player barely moves. The field of view also needs testing from 50–80 cm, with the user sitting below the sensor's axis.

Power

Staying under 500 mA

Peak draw can pass what a PC's USB 2.0 port allows. The firmware has to take turns between loads, and we haven't measured real currents yet.

Product

How do parents set it up?

The schedule, the phrases and the volume all need a setup flow. That could be a phone page over Wi-Fi or USB. None of it is designed yet, and it has to work without cloud accounts.

Product

Will a 14-year-old keep it plugged in?

It has to be annoying enough to work and charming enough not to end up in a drawer. Snooze limits, a "streak" reward and the tone of the voice lines are all untested.

Mechanical

Fastening, ears and the mic seal

The face cap and rear shell need screw bosses or snaps. The solid ears need coring out for moulding, and the rubber boot between the mic and the face port is a guess.

Electrical

No ESD or fuse on USB yet

A kid's desk is a hostile place. Add a USBLC6 and a PTC fuse, re-terminate the blower's lead to JST-SH, and confirm the AM312's pin order and the mic's stock.

Drawing

The GA drawing lags the model

Rev A shows a 25 mm blower, a 23 mm speaker and an INMP441 mic. The model and board moved to a 3010 blower, a 28 mm speaker and the ICS-43434. The drawing needs a rev B.
