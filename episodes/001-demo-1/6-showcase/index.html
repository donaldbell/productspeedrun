Backpack Defender

A fun motion-detecting alarm for a grade schooler's backpack or locker.

76 × 51 × 26 mm, deck-of-cards size 2 × AAA, pull one to disarm 5 alarm voices ≈ $5.40 electronics
Hands off my snacks! ♡

Concept art, chosen by the hosts in Stage 2. The engineered version below keeps the look but changes the insides: two AAA cells instead of three, an ESP32-C3 instead of an ESP-01, and no USB port.

Who it's for

For kids whose stuff keeps "wandering off"

School-age kids, roughly 6 to 12. It's a toy, not a security system: a cute guard with a loud, silly voice that makes a sneaky hand think twice, and makes the owner grin.

8:12 am · Locker 214

Lies flat on the shelf

It sits on its big flat side, face up. When the door swings open and a warm arm reaches in, the purple dome sees it and the alarm goes off. If it's the owner, one slap of the big button shuts it up.

12:30 pm · Lunch line

Clipped to a backpack

A charm loop on one end takes a keyring, a carabiner or a zip tie. The face looks out, and anyone unzipping the front pocket gets a robot shouting "INTRUDER" at them.

1:05 pm · Mid-lesson

Teachers can stop it in one move

The silence button is huge on purpose, so anyone can see how to stop it. For a full disarm, pop the battery door and take out a AAA. No app, no tools, no password.

How it behaves

Asleep almost all the time, loud when it counts

The firmware plan from Stage 5. Deep sleep is what lets two AAA cells last most of a school year.

ARMED

Snoozing

The ESP32-C3 sleeps at about 5 µA. The PIR or the button can wake it, and Wi-Fi stays off.

ALARM

Squeals

Motion wakes it. It turns the amp on, streams the chosen voice for about 10 s, then goes back to sleep.

SILENCE

Shh

One press stops the sound and ignores motion for a cool-down (about 60 s) so the owner can get their stuff.

SETUP

Hello, phone

Hold the button at power-on to start the hotspot and settings page. The choice is saved to flash.

What's inside

One small board, two AAA cells

A 70 × 45 mm two-layer board sits on four posts above the batteries. All 43 parts are on top, because the battery holder is just 0.5 mm below the board. It's fully routed, with zero ERC errors and zero DRC violations in KiCad 10.

Brain + Wi-Fi

ESP32-C3-WROOM-02

U2 · 18 × 20 × 3.2 mm

Hosts the hotspot settings page, plays tones over I2S, and wakes from deep sleep on a GPIO with no extra parts. It has native USB, so there's no serial chip.

~$1.90
Voice

MAX98357A + PS1240 piezo

U3 · BZ1

A filterless class-D amp takes the digital audio and drives a Ø12 mm TDK piezo, loudest near 4 kHz. Two 100 Ω resistors tame the edges.

~$1.45
Eye

AM312 mini PIR

U4 · Ø10 Fresnel lens

Near-field (3–5 m) with a digital output, drawing about 15 µA. An RC filter stops boost and Wi-Fi ripple from setting it off. Radar was rejected because it sees through locker walls.

~$0.80
Heart

TPS61023 boost

U1 · L1 1 µH

Two AAAs sag from 3.2 V to 2.0 V. The boost holds a steady 3.3 V and copes with 650 mA Wi-Fi peaks.

~$0.45
Kid-proofing

AO3401A guard FET

Q1 · SOT-23

Kids will put batteries in backwards. This one-cent P-FET shrugs it off and costs only ~40 mV of drop.

~$0.01
Big button

12 × 12 × 7.3 tact switch

SW1 · THT

Sits under the coral cap. A 0.4 mm pocket in the cap keeps it from pressing the switch all the time.

~$0.08

What's next

What we still don't know

This is a first pass from a speedrun. It's credible and buildable, but nothing has been printed, ordered or heard by an actual kid yet. Here's the honest list.

test first

Does a piezo sound fun enough?

A 12 mm piezo is loud near 4 kHz, but "musical" and "goofy" will come out thin and buzzy. A 20–28 mm micro-speaker fits the same pads for the same cost. We need an A/B test with real kids' ears.

test first

Does the PIR behave in a metal locker?

The dome looks straight up. It should trip on a door opening and a warm arm, but not when the locker warms up. Firmware could require two triggers within a few seconds.

before ordering

Check pinouts and part numbers

Check the AM312 pin order (it varies by vendor), the MAX98357A TQFN pinout and every LCSC number. Clean up the autorouted boost loop by hand, and confirm the fab can drill the 0.2 mm thermal vias.

before ordering

Fix the switch height mismatch

The enclosure is sized for a 7.3 mm tact switch, but the KiCad 3D model is an 8.5 mm part. Order the right one, or the plunger will dig 1.2 mm into the button.

enclosure

How does it stay together?

The lid, base and bottom plate aren't fastened yet (snap lip or screws?), and the battery door has no latch. Both matter for something that lives on a backpack.

enclosure

Inlays, pad print or sticker?

The face is printed as flush colour inlays for the prototype. Pad printing or a sticker would be much cheaper at volume.

product

Does it need a power switch?

Pulling a battery disarms it, as the brief asked. A $0.05 slide switch on the end is easy to add if teachers want one.

product

AAA or LiPo, and how cheap can it get?

We chose AAA so teachers can disarm it and nothing needs charging. Swapping the amp for an NS4168 saves about $1. A chip-down ESP32-C3 saves more, but needs RF work.
