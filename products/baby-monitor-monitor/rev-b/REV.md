### Snooze bar, bar light and boards (hosts' feedback, round 2)

Hosts' decisions: **keep the sensor's length** (the longer battery life is a bonus for parents). **No connect button:** pairing is a long press of the top button. And: *"make the top button larger and more accessible for sleep-deprived parents who need to push the button in dark conditions… something elongated like an alarm clock snooze bar… illuminated… a clear acrylic button and a color changing LED beneath it to indicate states."*

- **Snooze bar replaces the Ø13 button:** a 30 × 12 clear acrylic bar along the crown. Its top follows the crown 1 mm proud, with R1.8 rounded edges. A flange under the shell retains it, two plunger legs sit on two tacts, and a light leg runs down to an RGB LED. Tap = "I'm awake" (stop the buzz), hold 3 s = test / pair. *Why:* hosts' feedback (bigger, findable in the dark, pressable anywhere).
- **Two tacts in parallel** (SW1 moved (6, 0) → (10, 0); new SW3 at (−2, 0), both on BTN/IO7). *Why:* a long bar rocks on one switch, so a press at either end must still register. The 12 mm spacing is the least that fits the LED between the switch courtyards.
- **RGB bar light:** WS2812B-2020 (LED2) at (4, 0) on IO6. It's fed from the power switch's output through a 1N5819WS (D2), giving ~4.45 V on USB so 3.3 V data reads high; it's dark and draws nothing when switched off. R10 100 Ω on the data line, C10 1 µF, C11 100 nF. *Why:* hosts' feedback (illuminated, colour shows the state). The colour scheme is a firmware proposal in `5-electronics/NOTES.md`.
- **H2 post (12, −7.5) → (12, −9.5)** so the bar's flange clears it. Charger U1 turned landscape to (15.4, −5.6); D1, Q1, R3 and TP1 nudged. *Why:* room for SW1 at x 10 (fit request in `FIT.md`, done on both sides).
- **Sensor board re-placed and re-routed through the kit:** BTN and the status-LED anode got hand pre-routes; the clean route used Freerouting's default 40 passes (10 / 20 left 1–3 nets open). Final: **ERC 0, DRC 0 violations / 0 unconnected / 0 parity.**
- **Receiver board:** circuit unchanged. Its 3D model paths were fixed to revision depth (the routed `.kicad_pcb` edited, not re-routed, to keep the proven board), and the silk now reads "rev B". **DRC 0 / 0 / 0.**
- **Kit: true-size WS2812B-2020 stand-in** (`tools/kicad/models/WS2812B-2020.step`, 2.0 × 2.0 × 0.84). The Würth PLCC4 stand-in is 2.05 tall and made the fit check report a 0.2 mm clash with the bar's light leg that the real 0.84 mm LED doesn't have.
- **Model:** `iso.png`, `model.step`, `model.stl`, `model.glb` and `model.solidcraft` re-exported with the bar; `compare.png` still reads as the product; `progress-09.png` (bar close-up) and `progress-10.png` (section along the bar: legs over both tacts, light leg over the LED).
- **Kit fix (`tools/kicad/tidy_silk.py`):** non-plated holes (the mic's port) now count as obstacles for reference labels. It had put C9's label over the port (Rev A fixed that by hand).

### Firmware (7-firmware, track "code")

- **First firmware for both devices:** two PlatformIO projects, `7-firmware/sensor/` and `7-firmware/receiver/`, with the shared radio protocol and helpers in `7-firmware/common/`. *Why:* the code track's brief: sound level in, haptic intensity out, to wake the wearer.
- **ESP-IDF 5.5, not Arduino.** The pinned platform (`espressif32@6.12.0`) ships Arduino-ESP32 2.0.17, which has no ESP32-C6 support. Its ESP-IDF does support the C6.
- **`pins.h` generated from each board's `design.py`** (`common/scripts/gen_pins.py`, run before every build), with the C6-MINI-1 pad table checked against KiCad's symbol. Each `config.h` `#error`s if a net or pull the firmware relies on goes away.
- **Sensor:**
  - I2S at 32 kHz, band-limited to ~330 Hz–3.7 kHz, gives dB SPL every 250 ms.
  - A detector with an adaptive noise floor turns that into levels 0–3. Attack is 1 s of sound within 2 s, so slams don't count; release is one level per 6 s, so breaths don't end an alert.
  - Tap the snooze bar = "I'm awake" (a 3 min snooze, broken if the sound gets louder). Hold 3 s = test buzz + 30 s pairing offer.
  - The bar light follows the state scheme proposed in `5-electronics/NOTES.md`. The status LED is a VU meter.
- **Watch:**
  - Distinct ERM patterns, rising in strength (first pass 4 / 22 / 80 % mean drive; fussing is now glow-only, see below), battery-compensated so the 3 V motor feels the same from 4.2 to 3.5 V and is never over-driven.
  - An un-acked alert escalates one level every 45 s. It buzzes "link lost" if the sensor goes silent.
  - Radio duty-cycled (50 ms / 200 ms) with light sleep; the sensor repeats every packet so it lands in a wake window.
- **Host test** (`7-firmware/test/run_tests.sh`): the firmware's own `detector.c` and `haptic.c` on the Mac. Louder sound gives a stronger buzz, a door slam doesn't alert, the motor stays at or under 3 V. Writing it found a real bug: the breaths in a long cry let the noise floor creep up. The floor can now only fall during an episode.
- **`SYSTEM.md` written** (missing in Rev A and Rev B): the two devices, the ESP-NOW protocol, pairing without a watch button, and the power budget.
- **Hosts' decisions on the firmware (round 1):** fussing now **glows only** (no motor, and no escalation into a buzz; crying still escalates to screaming after 45 s); the 3 min snooze stays; the bar light gets **no night-off option** (its visibility is a feature); link-lost buzzing stays as is until bench testing. *Why:* the hosts' answers to the questions in `7-firmware/NOTES.md`.
- **Fixed: white noise that starts after the sensor is now learned** (bug found by the showcase track). The floor used to move only on windows below the fussing threshold, so a machine switched on later held the watch at "fussing" (52 dB) or "crying" (60 dB) all night. Now a sound that stays within 4 dB for 20 s becomes the floor, up to the floor's 62 dB ceiling, so an unbroken scream is never learned away. The host test now covers their exact case (52 and 60 dB after a quiet minute: learned after 20 s, still quiet 30 min later, crying over it still alerts) and a steady 75 dB scream (stays level 3). *Why:* the showcase track's report.
- **Three optional board requests** posted in `FIT.md` (`[open] 7→5`): VBUS sense on the sensor, a switched glow-LED supply and a charger sense on the watch. All are inside only. *Why:* the firmware can't tell plugged-in from full, and the dark WS2812B costs the watch ~5–10 mAh a night.

## Still open

- The sensor still reads longer and more tube-like than the concept's pebble: the 50 mm 103450 cell sets the length (question for the hosts in `4-model/NOTES.md`). The back recess reads as a big flat panel (pass 2's inset 3.0).
- Firmware needs a bench: sound thresholds (`7-firmware/sensor/src/levels.h`), buzz strengths (`receiver/src/haptic.c`), and the watch's real current with the radio duty-cycled (estimate ~11 mA, ~13 h on 150 mAh). How much to buzz when the sensor goes silent is left for bench testing (hosts).
- No ESP-NOW encryption and no OTA code yet (the partition table is ready for OTA).
- The status-light pipe is ~22 mm long: it crosses from the board LED at board y +7.5 to the −y cheek. If it looks dim on a real part, Stage 5 could move the LED to the −y half of the board near the front (it's near post H1 and the mic, so it's a layout question).
- Back panel layout: the concept has the slider top-left, a "connect" button top-right and the USB-C below centre. Rev B keeps the board's USB-C and slider side by side (and no connect button, per the hosts' Rev A decision). Moving them is a Stage 5 change.
- The watch is still 12.7 mm thick (concept ~10): set by the cell + board stack (see `4-model/NOTES.md`).
