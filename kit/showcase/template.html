Gamer Guardian

The monitor-top robot that keeps you in peak performance with helpful reminders.

It notices when you sit down, then nudges you to take a break, drink some water, grab a snack or sit up straight. It uses a puff of air and a friendly voice, so you never have to look away from the game.

What's inside

One 54 × 31 mm two-layer board stands upright behind the face. The front sees out through the visor; the back carries the brain, the audio and the power.

The brain

The ESP32 that can run Espressif's speech stack, a wake word plus about 200 commands, on the chip itself.

U5 · AM312

Presence

A tiny PIR that draws about 15 µA, on its own filtered supply so fan noise can't trigger it.

U4 · ICS-43434

Ears

A digital MEMS mic ported through a 1 mm hole in the board to a pinhole in the face.

U3 · MAX98357A

Voice

A class-D amp on 5 V drives a 28 mm, 1 W speaker. No codec, no filter.

Q1 · AO3400A

Breeze

Switches the blower at 25 kHz, too high to hear, and keeps it off at boot.

LED1–2 · WS2812B-2020

Eyes

Two 2 mm RGB LEDs on one data line, lit only when a reminder fires.

What we still don't know

This is a speedrun first pass. Here's what we'd answer before anyone orders a board or cuts a mould.

  • Board

    Routed and clean, but never built

    The first prototype has to confirm the PIR's pin order, the mic hole lining up with the face port, and the fit on the posts.

  • Voice

    Free-form speech needs a bigger chip

    Whisper-style transcription doesn't fit in the S3's RAM. The ESP32-P4 is the upgrade path, but it needs a Wi-Fi companion chip and a bigger head.

  • Feel

    Can you feel the breeze at arm's length?

    Small blowers move very little air. If it isn't noticeable at 50–70 cm on a quiet duty cycle, the fix is a 40 mm fan.

  • Sensing

    Does the PIR see a still, seated gamer?

    PIRs detect movement, and a focused player barely moves.
